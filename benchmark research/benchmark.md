# Benchmark Data: IT–Business Misalignment and Constrained IT in Real Organizations

## Executive Summary

The research below assembles defensible, named-source statistics on IT–business misalignment, the gap between IT operational delivery and strategic impact, shadow IT, SaaS waste, project value-realization failure, and the AI investment/implementation gap. Every datapoint is traced to an originating, reputable publisher (Gartner, McKinsey & Company / QuantumBlack, MIT CISR, Deloitte Insights, BCG, Flexera, IDC, S&P Global, Standish Group). I have flagged with confidence levels and excluded several widely circulated numbers (e.g., the often-cited "Gartner says shadow IT is 30–40% of IT spending in large enterprises") because I could not trace them to a primary Gartner publication on the open web — those should not be used in front of an expert audience.

---

## 1. How Common Is IT–Business Misalignment?

**Finding 1.1 — Only ~48% of digital initiatives meet or exceed business outcome targets.**
- Source: Gartner, *2025 CIO and Technology Executive Survey*, presented at Gartner IT Symposium/Xpo, October 2024.
- Sample: 3,186 CIOs/technology executives in 88 countries plus 1,126 non-IT CxOs, representing ~$17.6 trillion in revenue/budgets and $351 billion in IT spending.
- Confidence: **High** — direct Gartner press release with disclosed methodology and sample.

**Finding 1.2 — 50% of IT leaders cite "creating strategic alignment between IT and the business" as a top struggle.**
- Source: Gartner Peer Community, *IT Strategy One-Minute Insights* poll. While 96% claim some alignment, only 17% report "perfect alignment" between IT and business strategy.
- Confidence: **Medium** — Gartner-branded but practitioner-poll methodology rather than Gartner core research.

**Finding 1.3 — Gartner reports that, on average, only 22% of business technologists feel their organizations have a formal process for mapping IT spend to business outcomes (vs. 67% who say they discuss it).**
- Source: Gartner research cited in industry coverage (Software Improvement Group analysis of Gartner data). 
- Confidence: **Medium** — secondary citation of Gartner research; flag if used in front of academic audience without locating the original Gartner doc.

## 2. IT Projects Failing to Deliver Strategic Business Value

**Finding 2.1 — Large IT projects (>$15M initial budget) on average run 45% over budget, 7% over schedule, and deliver 56% less value than predicted. 17% threaten the existence of the company.**
- Source: McKinsey & Company in collaboration with University of Oxford, *"Delivering large-scale IT projects on time, on budget, and on value"* (study of 5,400+ large IT projects). Published in McKinsey Quarterly / McKinsey Digital, 2012, and still routinely cited by McKinsey.
- Confidence: **High** — peer-reviewed-quality methodology, jointly published with Oxford. Note: dataset is older (2012), but McKinsey continues to cite it; flag the date in the slide.

**Finding 2.2 — Across more than 800 BCG-studied digital transformations, only ~30–35% achieve their value targets. Approximately 70% of digital transformations fail to meet their objectives.**
- Source: BCG, *Flipping the Odds of Digital Transformation Success* (and reaffirmed in BCG's 2024 *Where's the Value in AI?*). McKinsey has reported similar figures in *"The State of Digital Transformation"*.
- Confidence: **High** — published BCG research with executive sample.

**Finding 2.3 — Standish Group CHAOS data: only ~31% of IT projects qualify as "successful" (on time, on budget, with satisfactory outcome); large projects succeed less than 10% of the time. After Standish redefined "success" to include customer-perceived value (replacing "on target") in 2015, success rates dropped a further ~7 points.**
- Source: Standish Group, *CHAOS Report* (1994–2020 series; final report 2020); database of ~50,000 projects across 1,000 organizations.
- Confidence: **Medium-High** — widely cited, but Standish methodology has been criticized in the academic MIS literature (e.g., Eveleens & Verhoef in IEEE Software, 2010). Use with attribution and a hedge.

## 3. Shadow IT — Business Units Bypassing IT

**Finding 3.1 — 41% of employees acquired, modified, or created technology outside IT's visibility in 2022; Gartner projects this rises to 75% by 2027.**
- Source: Gartner survey of 2,820 "business technologists" (2021/2022 Gartner research, cited in Gartner press releases and the 2023 Gartner Security & Risk Management Summit briefings).
- Confidence: **High** — directly attributed to Gartner with named research director (Chris Mixter, Darren Topham).

**Finding 3.2 — Of organizations with significant business-technologist activity, 85% have a team currently using high-impact shadow IT (e.g., deploying customer-facing systems outside official IT).**
- Source: Gartner research cited in CSO Online and Gartner-attributed industry coverage, 2023.
- Confidence: **Medium-High**.

**Finding 3.3 — Gartner: 69% of employees report intentionally bypassing cybersecurity guidance in the prior 12 months; business technologists are 1.8× more likely than other employees to behave insecurely.**
- Source: Gartner cybersecurity behavior research, 2023.
- Confidence: **High** — directly Gartner-attributed.

**Caveat:** I deliberately exclude the often-repeated claim that "shadow IT accounts for 30–40% of IT spending in large enterprises." That figure is widely repeated (Everest, BetterCloud, Cisco, etc.), but I could not locate a primary Gartner report or McKinsey publication establishing it. Do **not** use this in front of expert reviewers without a primary citation.

## 4. SaaS / Software Spend Waste

**Finding 4.1 — Gartner forecasts annual SaaS expenditure growth of 19%+, but ~25% of that software is underutilized or overdeployed. Other Gartner publications estimate ~30% of SaaS spend is "wasted" on unused licenses and features.**
- Source: Gartner, *Infographic: Why Are You Wasting Your SaaS Expenditure?* (Gartner document 4006574); separately Gartner research cited by NPI, Flexera, and Silicon.eu.
- Confidence: **High for the 25% figure** (directly traceable to a published Gartner infographic). The "30%" version is widely attributed to Gartner but appears in secondary citations more often than primary; safer to use the 25% figure.

**Finding 4.2 — Flexera *2024 State of ITAM Report* (n=503 IT professionals at 1,000+ employee firms): organizations self-estimate ~33% of SaaS spend wasted, ~33% IaaS/PaaS wasted, ~38% desktop software spend wasted. Even *advanced* ITAM practitioners report 20% SaaS waste, 30% desktop waste.**
- Source: Flexera, *2024 State of ITAM Report* (published June 2024). 2025 edition shows continued problem: 35% of respondents say SaaS waste *increased* year-over-year.
- Confidence: **High** — primary Flexera publication, transparent methodology, downloadable.

**Finding 4.3 — Forrester research: 94% of enterprises report overspending in the cloud.**
- Source: Forrester research cited in NPI Financial reporting.
- Confidence: **Medium** — directionally correct but the original Forrester report should be located if used as a headline number.

## 5. Gap Between IT Operational Performance and Strategic Impact

**Finding 5.1 — On average, organizations allocate 57% of IT budget to "support business operations," 26% to "incremental business change," and only 16% to "innovation."**
- Source: Deloitte, *2016–2017 Global CIO Survey* (1,271 CIOs), reported in Deloitte Insights, *"Driving value creation with technology investments."*
- Confidence: **High** — primary Deloitte publication. Note: dataset is from 2016–2017; flag as such, but Deloitte's 2025 *Tech Value* research confirms the pattern persists (most organizations believe they are missing 21–50% of expected returns from digital transformation).

**Finding 5.2 — Deloitte UK *Bridging the Tech Value Gap* (Sept 2025–Jan 2026 survey of 314 senior UK executives): the majority of business leaders feel there is 21–50% of untapped enterprise value in tech investments already made.**
- Source: Deloitte UK, *Bridging the Tech Value Gap* report (2026).
- Confidence: **High** — recent, primary Deloitte publication with disclosed methodology.

**Finding 5.3 — MIT CISR research on IT governance: firms with superior IT governance achieve **>25% higher profits** than firms with poor governance pursuing the same strategic objectives.**
- Source: Peter Weill & Jeanne W. Ross, *IT Governance: How Top Performers Manage IT Decision Rights for Superior Results* (Harvard Business Review Press; based on MIT CISR study of 250 enterprises worldwide).
- Confidence: **Very High** — peer-respected MIT CISR research published as an HBR Press book; this is the exact kind of source an information-systems professor will recognize.

**Finding 5.4 — MIT CISR research on "Future Ready" firms (study of digitally transformed organizations): top performers (highest margin) achieve ~70% effectiveness on each of four "transformation explosions"; the average firm sits well below this on all four.**
- Source: MIT CISR 2019 TMT and Transformation Survey (n=1,311), published in MIT CISR Research Briefings by Stephanie L. Woerner and Peter Weill, 2021.
- Confidence: **Very High**.

**Finding 5.5 — MIT CISR "Real-Time Business" research: top-quartile real-time-business firms achieve 62% higher revenue growth and 97% higher net profit margins than bottom-quartile firms; they are also 20% better at innovation, 22% better at operational efficiency, and 17% better at risk management.**
- Source: MIT CISR Research Briefing, *"What's Next: Top Performers Are Becoming Real-Time Businesses"* (Peter Weill et al.), reported by MIT Sloan in 2024.
- Confidence: **Very High**.

## 6. Early vs. Late IT Involvement and Project Success

**Finding 6.1 — When CIOs and CxOs *co-own* digital delivery (i.e., joint accountability through execution, not just sponsorship), 71% of digital initiatives meet or exceed business outcome targets — versus the 48% baseline. This is Gartner's "Digital Vanguard" cohort.**
- Source: Gartner, *2025 CIO and Technology Executive Survey* (n=3,186 CIOs + 1,126 CxOs), Oct 2024.
- Confidence: **Very High** — this is the single most defensible Gartner number on the alignment/early-involvement question.

**Finding 6.2 — Digital Vanguard CxOs dedicate 35% of their business-area staff to technology work (vs. 21% for other CxOs) and meet with their CIO four times more often.**
- Source: Same Gartner 2025 CIO Survey.
- Confidence: **Very High**.

**Finding 6.3 — McKinsey/Oxford research: top-performing IT-project teams differ from average performers primarily by (a) maintaining focus on strategic value beyond technical delivery, (b) detailed and sustained stakeholder engagement (business + IT executives, vendors, regulators) throughout the project lifecycle — *not* concentrated only at scoping. Projects that score well on "managing strategy and stakeholders" are far more likely to deliver expected value.**
- Source: McKinsey & Company / University of Oxford, *Delivering large-scale IT projects on time, on budget, and on value* (qualitative + quantitative analysis of large IT projects).
- Confidence: **High** — McKinsey-published, attributed framework ("value assurance"). Treat the directional claim as solid; the precise quantification of early-engagement uplift is McKinsey's recommendation rather than a single discrete percentage.

**Finding 6.4 — Standish CHAOS Decision Latency Theory: Organizations with average decision latency >3 hours are likely to revisit or reverse ~40% of their decisions. High-decision-latency teams achieve 18% project success vs. 91% for low-latency teams in agile environments.**
- Source: Standish Group, *CHAOS Report: Decision Latency Theory* (2018).
- Confidence: **Medium** — Standish methodology has academic critics; useful as a directional anchor.

## 7. What Separates "Strategic IT" Organizations from the Rest

**Finding 7.1 — Gartner's Digital Vanguard separators (2025 CIO Survey):**
- Co-ownership of digital delivery (CIO + CxO equally accountable end-to-end) — vs. traditional "IT delivery + business sponsor" model.
- Two-thirds of Digital Vanguard CIOs help business areas forecast their *own* digital-skill needs, vs. only 22% of other CIOs.
- They prioritize building the technology workforce *enterprise-wide*, not just within IT (only 16% of all CIOs do this; almost all Digital Vanguard CIOs do).
- They share technology leadership with other business areas (only 18% of all CIOs prioritize this).
- Source: Gartner press release, October 22, 2024; *2025 CIO and Technology Executive Agenda: Grow the Digital Vanguard*.
- Confidence: **Very High**.

**Finding 7.2 — McKinsey's "AI High Performer" / value-capture separators:**
- AI high performers are **2.8× more likely** to fundamentally redesign workflows around AI (55% vs. 20% of others).
- They are **3× more likely** to use AI for *transformative* business change rather than incremental improvements.
- They deploy roughly twice as many AI use cases as peers.
- They set growth/innovation objectives in addition to efficiency.
- They have committed senior-leadership ownership of AI.
- Source: McKinsey & Company / QuantumBlack, *The State of AI in 2025: Agents, Innovation, and Transformation* (n=1,993 participants in 105 countries; field period June 25 – July 29, 2025).
- Confidence: **Very High** — current, primary, large-sample.

**Finding 7.3 — MIT CISR research on the four pathways to "Future Ready" found that companies pursuing a *coordinated* multi-pathway transformation were significantly further along (59% complete) than companies pursuing multiple pathways without coordination (30% complete).**
- Source: MIT CISR 2017 Pathways to Digital Business Transformation Survey (n=400) and follow-up briefings by Woerner & Weill.
- Confidence: **Very High**.

## 8. AI Investment vs. AI Implementation Gap (2020–2026 Data)

**Finding 8.1 — 78% of organizations report using AI in at least one business function, but ~80% report **no significant bottom-line (EBIT) impact** — what McKinsey terms the "GenAI paradox."**
- Source: McKinsey & Company / QuantumBlack, *Seizing the Agentic AI Advantage* (June 2025), citing *The State of AI: How Organizations Are Rewiring to Capture Value* (March 2025).
- Confidence: **Very High**.

**Finding 8.2 — In McKinsey's 2025 State of AI survey, 88% of organizations now use AI in at least one business function. Only 39% attribute *any* EBIT impact to AI use, and most of those report less than 5% of EBIT attributable to AI. Nearly two-thirds (≈65%) have not yet begun scaling AI across the enterprise. Only **6%** are "AI high performers" capturing significant value.**
- Source: McKinsey & Company / QuantumBlack, *The State of AI in 2025*, published Nov 2025 (survey June–July 2025, n=1,993, 105 countries).
- Confidence: **Very High**.

**Finding 8.3 — ~90% of "vertical" (function-specific, transformative) gen AI use cases remain stuck in pilot phase. Only 1% of enterprises view their gen AI strategies as mature.**
- Source: McKinsey, *Seizing the Agentic AI Advantage* (2025) and *Superagency in the Workplace* (Mayer, Yee, Chui, Roberts, January 2025).
- Confidence: **Very High**.

**Finding 8.4 — BCG: only 26% of companies have moved beyond AI proofs-of-concept to generate tangible value; just 4% have cutting-edge AI capabilities consistently delivering significant value across functions. 74% struggle to scale AI value.**
- Source: Boston Consulting Group, *Where's the Value in AI?* (October 2024) — survey of 1,000 CxOs across 59 countries and 10 industries.
- Confidence: **Very High**.

**Finding 8.5 — IDC (in partnership with Lenovo): for every 33 AI proofs-of-concept an enterprise launches, only 4 reach production — an **88% pilot-to-production failure rate**.**
- Source: IDC / Lenovo, *AI CIO Playbook 2025*, with comments by Ashish Nadkarni (IDC Group VP).
- Confidence: **High** — primary IDC research, widely reported.

**Finding 8.6 — S&P Global: the share of enterprises abandoning *most* of their AI initiatives jumped from 17% in 2024 to **42% by end of 2024 / into 2025**.**
- Source: S&P Global Market Intelligence research, cited by The New York Times (Steve Lohr) and reported widely in 2025.
- Confidence: **High**.

**Finding 8.7 — Gartner predicts **30% of generative-AI projects will be abandoned after proof-of-concept by end of 2025**, and that **40% of agentic AI projects will be canceled by end of 2027** due to escalating costs, unclear business value, or inadequate risk controls.**
- Source: Gartner press release, July 29, 2024 (GenAI projects); Gartner press release, June 2025 (agentic AI).
- Confidence: **High** — these are *Gartner predictions*, not historical data — flag as forward-looking on the slide.

**Finding 8.8 — McKinsey: less than 30% of organizations report that their CEO directly sponsors the AI agenda — a key driver of stalled value capture.**
- Source: McKinsey, *State of AI 2025*.
- Confidence: **High**.

## Recommended Slide-Ready Framing

For an MBA audience and an ex-consultant IS professor, the strongest, most defensible single-slide narrative is built from these *primary*, recent, named-source datapoints:

1. **Misalignment is the norm, not the exception:** Only 48% of digital initiatives meet business outcome targets (Gartner, 2025 CIO Survey, n=3,186). Where CIOs and CxOs co-own delivery, that jumps to 71%.
2. **Operational success ≠ strategic success:** 57% of IT budgets go to "running" the business; only 16% to innovation (Deloitte CIO Survey). Most leaders believe 21–50% of expected tech-investment value remains untapped (Deloitte 2025/2026).
3. **Shadow IT is structural, not deviant:** 41% of employees already provision tech outside IT; Gartner projects 75% by 2027.
4. **Software waste is endemic:** Gartner pegs ~25% of SaaS spend as wasted; Flexera's 2024 ITAM data puts SaaS waste closer to 33%, with 38% on desktop software.
5. **AI is the new misalignment frontier:** 78–88% adoption, but only 6% of firms capture significant EBIT impact (McKinsey 2025); 88% of AI POCs never reach production (IDC); 42% of firms abandoned most AI initiatives in 2024 (S&P Global).
6. **Strategic IT separators (Gartner Digital Vanguard / McKinsey AI High Performers / MIT CISR Future Ready):** co-ownership of delivery, workflow redesign (not bolt-on), enterprise-wide tech-skill investment, tight CIO–CEO sponsorship, and superior IT governance (>25% higher profits per MIT CISR, Weill & Ross).

## Items I Deliberately Excluded (Quality Filter)

- The often-cited "Gartner: shadow IT is 30–40% of IT spending in large enterprises" — could not be traced to a primary Gartner publication; appears only in secondary tech-vendor blogs.
- The widely circulated "85% of AI projects fail" figure — typically attributed to Gartner but not verifiable in the original. The more rigorously sourced equivalents are McKinsey's 6% high-performer number, BCG's 26%/74% split, and IDC's 33-to-4 (88%) POC-to-production rate.
- The "70% of digital transformations fail" figure that is often attributed jointly to "BCG and McKinsey" without a specific citation — the sourced versions are BCG's 30–35% success rate from their 850-company study and McKinsey's "two-thirds have not begun scaling AI" finding. Use those primaries instead.
- The MIT NANDA "95% of GenAI pilots fail to deliver ROI" figure — appears in 2025 Forbes coverage but is from an MIT *initiative* paper (NANDA), not peer-reviewed work; flag carefully if used.
- Capterra, BetterCloud, and other SaaS-vendor survey statistics — useful directionally but not appropriate for an audience evaluating source quality.

All findings above can be cited directly from the named publishers with the year of publication noted; for a slide deck, I recommend prefixing each statistic with the source and year (e.g., "*Gartner, 2025 CIO Survey, n=3,186*") so the data holds up under scrutiny.
